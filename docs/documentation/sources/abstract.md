After a disaster, Government, Police, Emergency Medical Services and Fire agencies need a shared and current picture of damaged buildings, people who may need help, dangerous hazards and passable roads. Existing research and operational systems address these needs separately and rarely show how uncertain their outputs are. This report proposes RescueVision, a multi-agency platform designed to turn drone and satellite imagery into one shared incident map. Its six proposed features are before-and-after building damage assessment with an indicative restoration cost, 2D and 3D mapping with vehicle-specific rescue routes, detection of partly visible people from the air, person tracking with assistance cases, compound hazard alerts such as floodwater near live power lines, and approved reports that agencies can share. Every AI result is designed to keep its model version and to remain a candidate until a person confirms it. The report reviews the relevant literature, specifies 167 requirements and ten use cases, and presents an architecture, database and interface design traced to those requirements. A preliminary prototype shows dated satellite imagery of one earthquake-affected area in a common 2D view. No model accuracy or system performance has yet been measured; training, evaluation and user testing are planned for Project II.
